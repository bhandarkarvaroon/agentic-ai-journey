Insurance is a contract between a policyholder and an insurer.
A premium is the amount paid periodically to keep the policy active.
A deductible is the amount the policyholder pays before insurance covers the rest.
Collision coverage pays for damage to your vehicle from an accident.
Comprehensive coverage pays for theft, weather damage, and other non-collision events.
Liability coverage pays for damage you cause to other people or property.
A claim is a formal request to the insurer to pay for a covered loss.
A beneficiary is the person designated to receive insurance benefits.
An endorsement modifies the terms of an existing insurance policy.
A rider provides additional coverage beyond the standard policy.
Underwriting is the process of evaluating risk before issuing a policy.
A grace period allows extra time to pay a premium after the due date.
Coinsurance splits costs between the insurer and policyholder after the deductible.
An out-of-pocket maximum caps the total amount a policyholder pays in a year.
Gap insurance covers the difference between a car's value and the loan balance.
A copay is a fixed fee paid at the time of a healthcare service.
Subrogation allows the insurer to recover costs from a responsible third party.
Reinsurance is when an insurer buys coverage from another insurer.
An actuary uses statistics to calculate insurance risk and set premiums.
Loss of use coverage pays for temporary housing if your home becomes uninhabitable.
